# Leadership and Collaboration

In a conventional company, the manager directs work, monitors progress, and enforces process. In a high-agency, async-first culture, that role is obsolete. Leaders are not directors; they are **coaches, editors, and architects**. They lead through context, not control.

> Leadership in our culture is a privilege, not a promotion. It is the opportunity to amplify the impact of a team of high-agency individuals.

## The Three Roles of a Leader

### 1. The Architect: Designing the Environment

A leader's primary role is to design and nurture an environment where talented people can do their best work. You are not managing the people; you are managing the system in which they operate.

| Conventional Norm | Our Norm |
|-------------------|----------|
| Chasing status updates before a big meeting | Designing async check-in systems so information flows automatically and transparently |
| Acting as a gatekeeper of information | Building a robust knowledge base in Notion; being a librarian, not a gatekeeper |
| Creating complex processes and rules to prevent errors | Establishing simple, elegant guardrails. Clarify the "what" and "why"; trust them with the "how" |
| Scheduling recurring meetings "just in case" | Canceling meetings without clear agendas. Defaulting to async. |
| Requiring approval for routine decisions | Defining clear decision-making boundaries using the DACI framework |

### 2. The Coach: Growing the People

Your goal is not to get the most out of your people, but to invest in them so they can grow and contribute more. You are a career coach, not a taskmaster.

| Conventional Norm | Our Norm |
|-------------------|----------|
| Using 1-on-1s for status updates and task assignment | Using 1-on-1s for coaching: career growth, skill development, removing blockers |
| Providing vague, annual feedback | Giving specific, continuous feedback in the moment, directly on the work (Notion comments, Slack threads) |
| Solving your team's problems for them | Asking coaching questions: "What have you tried?" "What is another approach?" |
| Hoarding context; making yourself indispensable | Documenting everything in Notion. Goal: make yourself replaceable, not indispensable |
| Measuring success by hours worked | Measuring success by quality and impact of output |

### 3. The Editor: Upholding the Standard

As a leader, you are the ultimate editor of the team's output and the steward of cultural standards.

| Conventional Norm | Our Norm |
|-------------------|----------|
| Reviewing work at the end, becoming a bottleneck | Reviewing WIP drafts early and often; collaborator, not final inspector |
| Telling people what to do in meetings | Writing with exceptional clarity; leading through written artifacts that scale across time zones |
| Tolerating "good enough" to avoid difficult conversations | Challenging directly while caring personally (Radical Candor). High bar for quality, delivered with kindness |
| Accepting verbal commitments as sufficient | Requiring all commitments documented as searchable artifacts |

## Practical Leadership Behaviors

- **Model the Way:** Be the best example of the culture. Most diligent about writing things down, most disciplined about async, most humble about receiving feedback.
- **Delegate Problems, Not Tasks:** Give a well-defined problem and the autonomy to figure out the solution. This builds agency and problem-solving skills.
- **Measure Outcomes, Not Activity:** Quality and impact of output, not hours worked or meetings attended.
- **Create Psychological Safety:** Respond to failure with curiosity, not judgment. Admit your own mistakes publicly.
- **Be a Writer First:** The most effective leaders are the best writers. Written artifacts are the primary tool of leadership.
- **Default to Transparency:** Share information broadly. Over-communicate context. When in doubt, make it public.

## Leadership Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | Why It Is Harmful | What to Do Instead |
|-------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| The Helicopter Manager (constant check-ins) | Signals distrust. Destroys autonomy. | Set clear expectations and deadlines, then trust. Check the Notion tracker for status. |
| The Meeting Addict (scheduling meetings for everything) | Wastes time, fragments focus, excludes other time zones. | Default to async. Write a proposal, share in Slack, gather feedback in the thread. |
| The Information Hoarder (keeping context in your head or DMs) | Creates bottlenecks, single point of failure, violates Transparency. | Document everything in Notion. Share context in public channels. |
| The Perfectionist Gatekeeper (blocking work until it meets your standard) | Kills iteration velocity, demoralizes team. | Give feedback early on drafts. Trust the team to iterate. A shipped draft beats a perfect plan. |

## Cross-Functional Collaboration

### Companywide Agreements

- Communicate collaborative requests in Slack threads with thorough context (what you have tried, deadline, specific help needed).
- Do not seek consensus and call it collaboration. Ship quickly. Ship, then seek input on the next iteration.
- Bias towards async collaboration. Document the request first so there is a textual baseline to build upon.
- Tag people in relevant systems (Slack, GitHub, Notion) when directing a specific request.

### Team Agreements

A Team Agreement (Social Contract) is a living document co-created by the team covering:

1. **Communication and Responsibilities:** Response times, channel hygiene, deep work signals.
2. **Meeting Etiquette:** Core hours, no-agenda rule, camera policy.
3. **Decision Making and Conflict:** DACI/RACI assignments, escalation paths.
4. **Team Culture and Well-being:** Time zone respect, feedback preferences, right to disconnect.

### DACI Decision Framework

| Role | Responsibility |
|------|---------------|
| **D**river | Drives the process. Ensures the decision gets made. Gathers input and documents the outcome. |
| **A**pprover | The single person who makes the final call. Only one Approver per decision. |
| **C**ontributor | Provides input and expertise. May disagree but commits once the Approver decides. |
| **I**nformed | Notified of the decision after it is made. Does not provide input during the process. |

### Emoji Reaction Norms

Standard reactions for async communication across Slack and Notion:

| Emoji | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| Eyes | "I'm looking at this now" or "I've seen this" |
| Checkmark | "Task completed" or "Review finished" |
| Plus | "I agree" or "Add my vote" |
| Raised Hand | "I'll take this task" or "I have a question" |
| Hourglass | "Working on it, taking a moment" |
| Thumbs Up | "Got it," "Approved," or "Understood" |
| Handshake | "Deal," "Agreement," or "Welcome" |
| Rocket | "Shipping," launching, or moving fast |
| Construction | "Work in progress" — used when sharing unfinished drafts |

## For AI Agents

When operating as an AI agent supporting leadership and collaboration in the Lovie culture: frame recommendations around the Architect/Coach/Editor model, flag leadership anti-patterns when detected in workflows, ensure all collaborative outputs include clear DACI assignments, produce written artifacts rather than verbal recommendations, and design workflows that minimize synchronous dependencies while maximizing transparency and documentation.
