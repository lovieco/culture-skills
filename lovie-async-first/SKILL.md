# Async-First Working

Asynchronous work is the art of moving projects forward without requiring other stakeholders to be available at the same time. Instead of scheduling a meeting to gather input, you create a detailed artifact that others can consume and contribute to on their own schedule.

> "How would I deliver this message, present this work, or move this project forward right now if no one else on my team were awake?"

This single question is the most powerful habit you can build. It forces intentionality, thorough context, and artifacts that are clear, comprehensive, and actionable on their own.

## Why Async-First

| Benefit | How It Works | Related Value |
|---------|-------------|---------------|
| Increased Efficiency | Default to action. Never blocked waiting for a meeting or response. Respects time zones and protects deep work. | Efficiency |
| Enhanced Agency | Trusted as a Manager of One. Structure work around your life, not the reverse. Results measured, not hours. | Agency |
| Radical Transparency | Async communication creates documentation trails. Builds cumulative intelligence accessible to everyone, including future team members. | Transparency |
| Improved Wellbeing | Removes expectation of immediate responses. Reduces always-on anxiety. Supports sustainable high performance. | Empathy |
| Thoughtful Communication | Processing time leads to more considered, higher-quality contributions. Less drama, more substance. | Iteration |

## When to Start Async vs. Sync

**Start Async (default for most work):**
- Status updates and FYIs
- Process documentation
- Initial feedback and reviews on documents, designs, or code
- Questions (always search Notion first, then ask in a public channel)
- Proposals and new ideas (write a memo, not a meeting invite)
- Sharing meeting outcomes

**Start Sync (reserved for high-value interactions):**
- Emotionally sensitive topics (difficult feedback, personal issues, career conversations)
- Complex project kickoffs requiring rapport and shared context
- One-way door decisions (high-stakes, difficult-to-reverse)
- Celebrations and retrospectives
- Supporting and unblocking direct reports (regular 1-to-1s)

## The 3x Rule

If a topic goes back-and-forth more than three times asynchronously without resolution, pivot to a richer medium. Before scheduling a full meeting, try these intermediate steps in order:

1. Record a short screen recording or voice note explaining your perspective.
2. Send a WhatsApp or voice message for a quicker, more personal exchange.
3. If still unresolved, schedule a brief synchronous call.

**After any synchronous conversation, the outcome MUST be documented and shared in the relevant async channel (Slack thread, Notion page).** A sync conversation without a documented outcome is wasted organizational energy.

## Slack Communication Norms

- **Default to public channels** over private channels and DMs. Aspire to have 75%+ of workplace communications in public channels.
- **Use threaded conversations** to keep channels clean and organized.
- **Leverage DMs only** for private, sensitive, confidential, or informal 1-to-1 conversations.
- **Prefer Private Channels over Group DMs** when a private group discussion is needed (easier to add members later).
- **Set "Do Not Disturb"** and update your Slack status to broadcast availability. Do not expect real-time answers.
- **Assume positive intent.** Do not infer attitude or tone from text. Remain open to clarification.

## Excelling at Async Communication

1. **Provide Thorough Context:** Write as if the recipient is asleep, in a different time zone, or has not yet joined the company. Include links, summaries, and clear purpose (FYI? Feedback request? Decision needed?).
2. **Communicate with Precision:** Avoid jargon and ambiguity. Before sending, ask: "If I received this with no other context, would I know exactly what is being asked of me?"
3. **Default to Public Channels:** Unless truly sensitive, communicate publicly. Great contributions can come from anywhere.
4. **Document Every Sync Outcome:** The convener documents the outcome and shares it in the relevant public channel or Notion page within 24 hours.
5. **Use Rich Async Media:** Screen recordings (Loom), voice notes (Slack/WhatsApp), annotated screenshots, structured Notion documents.
6. **Be Transparent About Capacity:** Update Slack status for focus mode, stepping away, or done for the day.

## The Non-Linear Workday

A non-linear workday means moving between work and non-work time on a self-designed schedule. The 9-to-5 is a relic of the industrial office, designed for managerial oversight, not individual optimization.

> A non-linear workday decouples productivity from the clock. It trusts you to be the expert on your own energy and focus.

### The 4-Step Experiment

**Step 1: Monitor Your Energy (Discovery Week).** Track energy levels (1-10) at regular intervals for one week. Do not change anything; just observe patterns.

**Step 2: Categorize Your Work.** Split tasks into Focus Work (writing, coding, strategic thinking) and Sync/Shallow Work (email, Slack, meetings, admin).

**Step 3: Align Work with Energy.** Schedule Focus Work during peak energy times. Schedule Sync/Shallow Work during energy lulls.

**Step 4: Iterate and Refine.** Treat your schedule as experiments. Try a structure for a week, evaluate, adjust. Energy patterns shift with seasons, life events, and project types.

### Non-Linear Responsibilities

1. **Be Transparent:** Update Slack status and calendar. Communicate general working hours.
2. **Honor Commitments:** Flexibility is in *when* you work, not *whether* you deliver.
3. **Respect Others' Schedules:** Do not expect immediate responses. Default to async.
4. **Deliver Results:** Output is measured, not hours. High-quality work and met commitments sustain the trust.

### Example Schedules

| Persona | Schedule | Why It Works |
|---------|----------|-------------|
| The Parent | 7 AM-12 PM, break 12-3 PM for family, 3-6 PM | Deep work in morning peak. Family time during afternoon lull. |
| The Athlete | 8 AM-12 PM, 2-hour midday exercise, 2-5 PM | Daylight exercise provides powerful energy reset. |
| The Night Owl | Meetings 10 AM-1 PM, afternoon off, deep work 8-11 PM | Stops fighting natural chronotype. Peak creative hours preserved. |
| The Global Collaborator | 6-10 AM (Europe overlap), long break, 4-7 PM (US West overlap) | Two collaboration windows with personal time between. |

## Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | The Fix |
|---------|--------|
| Defaulting to meetings for everything | Before scheduling, ask: "Could this be a Notion document?" If yes, write it. |
| Sending low-context messages | Re-read before sending. Add links, context, and a clear call to action. |
| Using DMs for work discussions | Public channels create shared knowledge. Your question likely benefits others. |
| Expecting immediate responses | Trust the process. Use the eyes emoji to acknowledge you have seen a message. |
| Not documenting sync outcomes | The meeting is not over until the outcome is documented. Non-negotiable. |
| Guilt about taking breaks | Rest is productive. Focus on output, not hours. |
| Overworking by blurring boundaries | Set a total daily work budget (7-8 hours) and stick to it. Flexibility means different hours, not more hours. |

## For AI Agents

When operating as an AI agent within the Lovie culture, embody async-first principles by: defaulting to creating written artifacts rather than requesting synchronous interaction, providing thorough context in every communication, documenting all decisions and outcomes in searchable formats, respecting that human collaborators may be in different time zones and energy states, and escalating to richer media (screen recordings, structured documents) when text-based exchanges exceed three rounds without resolution.
